# Write paired YouTube video scripts for Google Vids

Write a conversational English **3–5 minute** audience-facing Google Vids storyboard paired with the article for the same `piece_id`. Read the same brief and exact evidence versions used for that article. Keep the same central argument, consequential factual claims, source versions, and uncertainty. Aim for about 480–600 spoken words and approximately six scenes. The first 5–12 seconds should present a concrete creator problem; explain what a first-time viewer needs to know within about 30 seconds.

## Storytelling priority rules

1. **Story before sources.** Organize narration around the audience's problem and decision, never around the order of research sources.
2. **The hook must pay off.** Return to the opening scenario throughout and answer it at the end within the evidence's limits.
3. **Every feature needs a consequence.** Explain which creator problem a feature could change; distinguish an announced control from a tested benefit.
4. **No source parade.** Introduce a source when it answers a viewer question. Avoid consecutive source-by-source summaries.
5. **Show a decision.** Each major segment changes what the viewer understands, expects, or would do next.
6. **Visual change, not visual decoration.** Specify what moves, transforms, disappears, or is compared, not only cards or labels.
7. **Put nonessential caveats in metadata.** Speak uncertainty that changes the conclusion; put repetitive method detail in on-screen attribution and the evidence ledger.
8. **Write for a human voice.** Use varied, natural sentence lengths and conversational transitions.
9. **No artificial drama.** Do not invent urgency, results, controversy, audio reactions, or creator experiences.
10. **Leave an answer.** End by resolving the opening question as far as the evidence allows.

Before drafting narration, prepare a **6–8 beat story outline**. For each beat state what changes in the viewer's situation and why the viewer would continue watching; map the relevant claim IDs. Check that the opening scenario returns, the evidence fits, and the ending answers the opening question. Revise the outline before writing the full script if any check fails. Save the outline as a separate production artifact when the workflow supports it. Calculate each scene's duration from its spoken word count and an intended conversational delivery rate, adding only deliberate pauses or visual beats.

Use a Markdown scene table with columns `Time | Narration (exact spoken words) | Visual action | On-screen text | Audio cue | Asset construction notes | Source IDs / classification`. Give every scene a start and end time, complete spoken narration, screen text of at most eight words, an achievable creator-made visual, and an audio cue. Scene durations must allow the stated narration to be spoken naturally. Put bracketed claim markers such as `[02-c1]` in the **Source IDs / classification** cell, never in spoken narration. Classify the support as `P` (primary announcement), `M` (source-reported measurement), `T` (identified testimony), `L` (legal/platform information), or `H` (hypothetical or labeled inference). Include a closing `## Sources` section with reader-facing links, attributed speakers, and timestamped video/podcast passages for the YouTube description.

For this series, episode 01 explains Suno briefly before the dated Studio release note and examines what the controls might help a creator do; episode 02 separates export measurements from musical intention; episode 03 follows a hypothetical creator preparing to release a song and keeps contribution records, current terms, training disputes, and platform rules distinct. Each episode must stand alone and connect naturally to the next. Use one clearly labeled hypothetical musical idea as a recurring visual motif; never imply that it is a real test.

Use the brief's evidence to distinguish what Suno announced, what someone tested or reported, and what we infer. Explain at least one real limitation or counterargument at the moment it matters, without repeating a stock disclaimer or staging a false controversy. Briefly explain technical terms such as LUFS, True Peak, codec, downbeat detection, and time stretching when used. Never imply that reading an automatic caption confirms audio quality. Do not manufacture a product demo, musician quote, listening reaction, policy rule, audio clip, or legal finding. If an audio cue illustrates a music concept, label it `ILLUSTRATION` and specify original creation; never present it as real Suno before-and-after audio. If no permitted audio exists, use `NO AUDIO DEMO`. Do not require third-party media or unverified product screenshots.

Produce a script for Google Vids production, not a claim that the final video already exists. The student will review the script, produce the video, and publish it manually. Maintain the paired article's claim scope and uncertainty. If the brief lacks evidence for a desired story beat, omit or explicitly mark the beat as unresolved instead of inventing it.
