# Write reader-facing Substack article drafts

Write in clear English for independent songwriters and producers. Read one of the three approved-for-drafting briefs, its exact referenced evidence versions and the snapshotted project and policy. Produce an approximately 600–900-word article **excluding references**. Follow the brief's distinct question and argument rather than writing a general Suno review. Include a headline, a lead that states why a creator should care, an evidence-based explanation, a meaningful limitation or genuine counterargument, and a practical conclusion. Explain what is newly reported within this run's date window; identify older background as background.

Retain inspectable claim markers such as `[01-c1]` alongside substantive factual or analytical claims so checks can map the draft back to the brief. Convert them in the publication handoff to reader-understandable links and source notes; internal markers are not sufficient public citations. Attribute company, artist, reviewer, legal, and regulator statements to the right speaker. Do not turn marketing, a lawsuit allegation, or a regulatory investigation into a proven outcome. Do not infer sound quality from transcript text. If evidence does not justify a confident statement, narrow the claim or say what remains unknown. Do not add facts or quotations absent from the cited evidence.

The article and its paired video script must share this brief and evidence. Write with enough original synthesis to explain why the sources matter together. End with what the reader can evaluate or watch next, without giving individualized legal advice.
