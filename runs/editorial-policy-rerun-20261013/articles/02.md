# What Suno Quality Tests Actually Measure

*Evidence as of 2026-10-13 UTC. Draft from a pilot run; recheck dates before publication.*

If you are deciding whether a Suno-assisted track is ready to release, a verdict that one model “sounds better” may be less useful than knowing what was tested. The recent comparisons in this brief examine loudness, peaks, frequency content, melody, and perceived vocal clarity. Those are different questions. Taken together, they offer ways to inspect your own output, but they do not establish a general quality score for Suno v6. [02-c6]

## What the measurements can show

In a September 20 comparison, WaveDisco’s author generated three songs across four model versions, keeping the lyrics and prompt the same for each song. The author measured LUFS and True Peak through the same analysis pipeline. The numbers moved, but the author found no consistent direction in those measures that would support an overall improvement claim. That is useful evidence about those outputs and measurements; it does not tell us which recording a listener would prefer. [02-c1]

WaveDisco also compared WAV and MP3 exports where both were available. For the three v6 variants, the author reported zero LUFS difference between formats, while stressing that the format comparison covered only two songs. A creator could take that as a reason to check the formats they plan to use. It would be too much to assume their next export will behave the same way, or that matching loudness makes two files equally satisfying to hear. [02-c2]

A September 25 test by ytyng asked a different question. Its author reported that the v6 exports had 2 to 3.4 dB less energy from 2 to 12 kHz and a 36% lower spectral centroid than the older set. The author used an FFT after decoding the files, but also disclosed two major limits: the songs, genres, and prompts differed between sets, and the v6 and earlier files used different codecs. The measured difference is therefore a finding about those sets of files. It cannot, on its own, isolate what changing the model version did. [02-c3]

These results need not agree or disagree. LUFS and True Peak address level and peaks; ytyng examined the spectrum. Neither measurement says whether a generated part serves your song. Nor can a frequency comparison settle whether the result followed an instruction you gave it. That distinction matters when your concern is a particular vocal, melody, or arrangement rather than a model ranking. [02-c6]

## What a creator demonstration can show

The October 9 video *Can You Direct Suno V6 In Your Lyrics?* approaches control through a creator’s listening judgment. The creator says they used the same style for another attempt and later judged one version to have more vocal clarity. They also acknowledge that the perceived difference might simply be Suno producing another variation. This makes the attempt worth considering as something to try with your own song, but it does not prove that the added direction caused clearer vocals. [02-c4]

In another October 9 video, Andrea Olsen of Produce Like a Boss asks whether Suno kept the melody in her test and answers yes. That is evidence of her judgment about that result, not a measured rate at which Suno preserves creators’ melodies. The brief also identifies a music-business training promotion in her video, relevant context when weighing a creator’s assessment. It does not make her observation false; it gives readers a reason to keep the observation tied to the particular example she presented. [02-c5]

There is a genuine case for using these demonstrations: an independent musician may care more about whether a tool follows *their* idea than about a population average. Yet each video involves an individual song and the creator’s own judgment. The supplied evidence does not include a broad independent comparison with matched prompts, settings, export formats, and listener ratings. We still cannot say how often another songwriter would get a result they felt ready to release. [02-c4] [02-c5] [02-c6]

For your next experiment, choose the property that would make or break your track, then inspect the actual exports you might use. If it is level, compare level; if it is the melody or a vocal direction, listen for that in each result. Keep track of what you changed so a promising variation does not become mistaken for a proven control. The question these tests leave open is the one only your intended release can answer: does this specific output carry your idea well enough for you to stand behind it? [02-c6]
