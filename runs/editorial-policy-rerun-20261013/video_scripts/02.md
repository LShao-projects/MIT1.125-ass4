# What Suno Quality Tests Actually Measure — Video Script, Piece 02

*Evidence as of 2026-10-13 UTC. Draft from a pilot run; recheck dates before publication.*

**Target length:** About 4 minutes. Visuals are production suggestions; no product footage or audio is assumed.

## 0:00–0:35 — The question

**Narration:** If you make your own songs, “Is Suno v6 better?” is a reasonable question. But better in what sense: loudness, tonal balance, how closely it follows your melody, or whether you would release the result? The tests in this piece examine different parts of that question. None gives us an overall quality score. [02-c6]

**Visual:** Creator-made diagram with four branches: *levels*, *spectrum*, *melody*, *directions*.

## 0:35–1:25 — What the level test found

**Narration:** WaveDisco’s author regenerated three songs across four model versions, keeping the lyrics and prompt the same. They measured LUFS and True Peak on each output. The numbers moved, but the author found no consistent direction that would support saying v6 sounded better overall. That is the author’s finding about these measurements, not a listening verdict. [02-c1]

For two songs with both export formats available, the author also reported no LUFS difference between WAV and MP3 across the three v6 variants tested. The author cautioned that this was an illustration from two songs, and other tracks may behave differently. [02-c2]

**Visual:** A simple grid: three songs × four versions. Then show *LUFS* and *True Peak* as measurement labels. Keep any chart schematic; do not invent values.

## 1:25–2:15 — What the spectrum test found

**Narration:** Ytyng asked a different question about the sound’s spectrum. In the author’s exports, the v6 set measured 2 to 3.4 decibels lower between 2 and 12 kilohertz, with a 36 percent lower spectral centroid. [02-c3]

That result needs a large qualification. Ytyng says the v6 side contained two songs and the older side six, with different prompts. The sets also used different codecs: Opus for v6 and MP3 for the older exports. Those differences mean the measurement cannot isolate what changing the model version caused. [02-c3]

**Visual:** Neutral spectrum illustration labeled *reported measurement, not a controlled model comparison*. Do not present an invented waveform or listening example.

## 2:15–3:15 — What creator demonstrations add

**Narration:** Control is another question again. In a YouTube demonstration, the creator of *Can You Direct Suno V6 In Your Lyrics?* says they used the same style, judged one version clearer, and later acknowledged that ordinary generation variation might explain the change. It is a workflow someone could try, not proof that the directions caused the improvement. [02-c4]

Andrea Olsen of Produce Like a Boss tested one of her own voice memos. She judged that Suno kept its melody, while still hearing a result she identified as AI. That tells us about her input and her judgment, not how reliably Suno will preserve another musician’s melody. Olsen also promotes music-business training in the video, a commercial interest to keep in mind when weighing her assessment. [02-c5]

**Visual:** Creator-made flow diagrams: *same style → separate generations → creator judgment* and *voice memo → generated output → creator judgment*. Do not imply that captions verify the sound.

## 3:15–4:00 — What to do with the evidence

**Narration:** These findings can coexist. WaveDisco measured loudness and peaks; ytyng measured spectral properties; the video creators judged whether specific musical intentions came through. One test cannot answer the others’ questions. [02-c6]

If you are considering a release, choose the property that matters for your song, listen to and inspect your own exported result, and compare it against your original intention. That is a practical inference from these different methods, not a guarantee of release readiness. We still lack a broad, matched test with listener ratings across creators and genres, so overall musical quality remains an open question. [02-c6]

**Visual:** Closing checklist: *My melody? My directions? My export? My release standard?* End card: *Test the song you intend to release.*
