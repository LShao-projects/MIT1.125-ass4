# Episode 2 — clearer story proposal

This is a proposed editorial revision for the existing Google Vids draft. It does not alter the approved script, the Tova narration, or the current video. Its evidence base remains the approved first-run package.

## One question

**You brought a melody to Suno and got several candidate versions. How do you decide which one is worth continuing or sharing?**

The episode's answer: first judge whether a version preserves the musical idea you meant to make; then check the exact audio file you would send or release. Recent comparisons can inform those checks, but they cannot declare a universal winner for your song.

## Why this is the second episode

Episode 1 asks what Suno's recent tools let a creator try. Episode 2 asks whether the resulting version works for that creator. Episode 3 begins only after the creator has chosen a version and asks what to verify before publication.

## Proposed viewer journey

1. **Start with the creator's decision.** A songwriter records a short original melody and uses Suno to develop it. The video shows two *fictional, clearly labeled* candidate exports: A looks louder on an illustrative meter; B follows the drawn melody more closely. These are illustrations, not Suno outputs or a listening test. The question is which version the songwriter should keep working on, not which model is universally best.
2. **Check the music first.** Ask whether the melody, vocal direction, and arrangement still serve the songwriter's idea. Andrea Olson reports that one Suno result kept much of her uploaded voice-memo melody, while she still heard an AI quality. The On a Creative Journey creator liked a later vocal but also acknowledged repeated generations and Studio cleanup. These are useful examples of *what those creators listened for*, not average performance or proof that one prompt caused an improvement.
3. **Check the actual file second.** A promising version still needs an export check before it is sent out. WaveDisco measured loudness and True Peak in three songs across four model versions and did not find one consistent v5.5-to-v6 direction. Its WAV/MP3 comparison included only two songs with both formats. The practical advice is to inspect the particular export you will use; the report cannot predict that export's quality.
4. **Explain why a chart cannot settle the choice.** ytyng reported less high-frequency energy in the v6 files it examined, but its compared songs, prompts, and codecs differed. Its chart describes those files and does not isolate the model as the cause. Put this caveat beside the chart immediately, then return to the songwriter's two versions.
5. **End with an actionable decision.** Keep the version that best serves the original musical idea, and check that version's final exported file. Save the original memo, generated alternatives, format, and later edits so the comparison remains understandable. This is our editorial checklist derived from the sources, not a tested Suno feature or a universal quality score. Once the songwriter has a version they may release, Episode 3 takes over.

## Single sentence to say early and repeat at the end

“A meter can describe your exported file; only you can decide whether the music still follows your idea. You need both checks before sharing the song.”

## Evidence boundaries to preserve

- No actual Suno A/B audio or real measured export appears in our video. Keep all A/B graphics labeled as hypothetical illustrations.
- Attribute the measurements to [WaveDisco](https://blog.wavedisco.com/blog/suno-v6-vs-v55-audio-test) and [ytyng](https://www.ytyng.com/en/blog/suno-v6-review-variety-max-mode-spectrum), with their small samples and mismatched-input limits visible near the respective claim.
- Attribute the process accounts to [On a Creative Journey](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3l1FvHVuLnU) and [Andrea Olson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nl-dGFi5ebw). Olson promotes her own program, and neither person's result is representative of all songwriters.
- Do not claim that v6 generally sounds better or worse, that a louder file is a better song, or that a reported spectrum difference was caused by the model alone.
