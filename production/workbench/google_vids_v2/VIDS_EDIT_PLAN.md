# Suno in Practice — cinematic edit plan

**Status:** Visual direction approved by the student. This is an edit handoff for the three approved first-run scripts, not a published video. All still photographs in this directory are generated illustrations of a fictional songwriter. The real claims, dates, source links, and exact narration remain in [`runs/live-20261013T181427Z/package/video_scripts/`](../../../runs/live-20261013T181427Z/package/video_scripts/).

## Shared visual rules

- English spoken narration. Three separate 16:9, 3–5 minute videos, with their paired articles using the same approved evidence. Do not substitute second-run drafts without a new review.
- Open in one recognizable workspace and return to the songwriter's unresolved decision at the end of each episode. Cut between wide, hands, notebook, and blurred screen details every few seconds; a 30–50 second narration row should never hold on one unmoving image.
- Use short editorial captions only when they answer the scene's question. Keep the picture itself cinematic and uncluttered. Use the question cards in the approved script's “On-screen text” column as the primary caption copy.
- Every reconstructed scene carries a small **“ILLUSTRATION · NOT SUNO UI”** label. The label is already baked into the rough-cut previews, and must also be present in any Vids assembly using these images. No generated picture is evidence of Suno behavior, sound quality, user terms, or a real upload.
- Source attribution appears on screen when a named person, test, regulator, or platform claim enters. The video description must carry the source URLs in each script. No third-party audio or video is used.
- The video starts with a small “Evidence as of October 13, 2026” label. Recheck relative dates, site terms, and platform status before publication; later developments can require updating the approved script.
- Use only original or licensed music and effects if added. The current rough cuts are intentionally silent and must not be mistaken for the final videos.
