# Episode 2 — Which Suno version is worth keeping?

*Revision for the private Google Vids draft. Evidence as of October 13, 2026. A and B are hypothetical illustrations, not Suno outputs or a listening test. Narration uses Tova at the existing slow pace.*

## Scene 1 — The songwriter's choice

Imagine you recorded a melody, then used Suno to develop a demo. Now you have two possible versions. In our drawing, A looks louder on a meter; B follows the melody you wrote. These are imaginary versions. We did not run this song through Suno. So how would you decide which one is worth keeping? That is the question for this episode. When someone says a Suno version is “better,” better for what? Let's begin with your music, then check the file you would share.

*Visual:* The same hand-drawn melody enters two clearly labeled fictional branches. A meter appears beside A, while the melody line remains visible beside B. Hold the unresolved choice. On screen: “Which one serves your song?” and “Illustration — no Suno audio.”

## Scene 2 — What did you mean to make?

First, listen for what you meant to make. Is your melody still there? Does the vocal go in the direction you wanted? Does the arrangement help the song? In one YouTube demonstration, On a Creative Journey tried more detailed lyric directions and liked a later vocal. But the creator also made repeated generations and cleaned up the result in Studio. We can see a process. We cannot tell which single step made the difference. Compare each version with your original sketch, not only with the other version.

*Visual:* An original melody sketch stays fixed while several fictional generation and edit paths branch beneath it. Label the source's account without depicting its actual song or interface. On screen: “Does it keep your idea?” and “One creator's account.” Sources: [02-c4], [02-c6].

## Scene 3 — One voice memo, two judgments

Songwriter and producer Andrea Olson runs the YouTube channel Produce Like a Boss. In one demonstration, she uploaded a voice memo. She said Suno kept much of its melody, but the result still sounded AI-like to her. Those are two different judgments: did it follow your idea, and is it finished? Her test covers one memo, not yours. She also promotes her own program, so keep that context in mind. If B follows your melody, it may be worth editing, even if A looks louder.

*Visual:* A generic voice-memo icon feeds a fictional melody lane. Two questions appear in sequence: “Melody retained?” and “Ready to use?” Return to A/B with B still under consideration, not marked a winner. On screen: “Andrea Olson · songwriter and producer · individual demonstration.” Sources: [02-c5], [02-c6].

## Scene 4 — Check the chosen export

Now check the version you would actually share. WaveDisco generated three songs with the same lyrics and prompt across four Suno model versions, then measured loudness and True Peak, or brief signal peaks. Those numbers describe exported files; they do not score the songs. The measurements did not move consistently from v5.5 to v6. Its separate WAV-versus-MP3 comparison had only two songs with both formats. So do not pick A because its fictional meter looks stronger. Check the chosen export's level, peaks, and format before you send it.

*Visual:* The chosen fictional version becomes a file card. A level ruler, peak indicator, and WAV/MP3 labels appear one at a time. Put “3 songs / 4 versions” and “2 paired formats” beside the corresponding reported measurements. On screen: “Measure the actual file.” Sources: [02-c1], [02-c2], [02-c6].

## Scene 5 — Why a chart cannot choose for you

Another tester, ytyng, reported less high-frequency energy in the v6 files they examined. That might sound like a verdict on sound quality. But the earlier and later files used different songs, prompts, and audio encodings. Several things changed at once. The chart describes those exports; it cannot show that the model alone caused the difference. If your chosen version sounds dull, compare the actual exports under matched conditions when you can. These reports give you things to check. They do not prove that v6 is universally better or worse.

*Visual:* Draw one reported curve, then reveal the changed song, prompt, and codec labels. Keep the cause box unresolved. Return immediately to the creator's chosen file. On screen: “Observed difference ≠ proven cause.” Sources: [02-c3], [02-c6].

## Scene 6 — A decision the creator can use

So go back to your own A and B. First, which one keeps your musical idea: your melody, vocal direction, and arrangement? Then, for that version, does the exact file you will share behave as expected? Save your original memo, the generations you compared, the export format, and any later edits. That way you know what changed. This two-part check is our practical synthesis of four recent reports, not a universal verdict on Suno. Once you find a version you may release, one more question appears: what should you verify before you press Publish?

*Visual:* Resolve the choice into two successive gates: “1. Your music” and “2. Your export.” Put the chosen version in a release folder and leave Publish untouched for Episode 3. On screen: “Keep the idea. Check the file.” Sources: [02-c1]–[02-c6].
