## A. Series map

**Promise:** Follow one clearly hypothetical independent musician from a promising musical idea, through editing and evaluation, to a release decision. At each stage, separate what sources establish from what the musician still needs to test or check.

**Viewer:** English-speaking independent songwriters and producers who make demos or releases and are considering Suno as one step in their workflow.

**Recurring motif:** An illustrated song timeline and folder. The same *hypothetical* phrase lands late in episode 1, is evaluated in episode 2, and sits beside a hypothetical upload page in episode 3. The graphics never purport to show a real Suno test or product interface. A short original transition sting may connect episodes; no third-party audio is needed.

| Episode | Job and promise | Transition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Discover | Explain Suno and Studio briefly, then ask what the October editing controls might help a creator do. | Editing raises the question of whether the result is better. |
| 2 — Evaluate | Separate export measurements from fidelity to the creator's musical intention. | A result that passes the creator's test still needs release checks. |
| 3 — Release | Turn the release decision into checks for contribution records, applicable terms, destination rules, and evolving platform classification. | Ends with a decision framework, without a legal verdict. |

## Audience and source role

The audience is English-speaking independent songwriters and producers who make demos or releases, know basic recording and editing, and are considering Suno as one tool. The recurring *illustrated* creator has written a melody, is building a demo, and must decide whether to edit, evaluate, or release a Suno-assisted result. Each episode should give this person a concrete decision, not a product tour.

| Episode | Creator's practical question | What recent evidence contributes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | “Can I keep my musical idea while fixing timing, length and exit?” | Suno's dated update establishes what controls exist; independent creator accounts show editing and sampling workflows, while leaving the new controls untested. |
| 2 | “Which version is suitable for my demo or release?” | Recent measurements constrain claims about the exported file; creator accounts show how musical intention is judged in individual workflows. |
| 3 | “What must I know before I publish this track?” | Recent creator accounts, a terms investigation, licensing reports and a planned platform filter identify distinct questions; they do not settle rights or predict reach. |

The sources are **evidence used in the argument**, not background decoration. The article must link substantive claims to evidence; the video should attribute consequential claims naturally and put clear references, including media timestamps, in its YouTube description. The 30-day window is checked against the actual run date. Each publication states its evidence as-of date; the videos include that date on screen without making the narrator recite every source date.
