# What Suno quality tests can actually tell you

**Watch the companion video:** https://youtu.be/6avganzCYio

*Evidence as of 2026-10-13 UTC.*

If you are deciding whether a Suno-assisted song is ready to release, “Does v6 sound better?” is too broad a test. You need to know whether your exported file behaves as you expect and whether the song follows the idea you brought to it. Four recent creator reports offer ways to inspect those questions, but none establishes a typical result for independent musicians.

The evidence published for this piece between September 20 and October 9, 2026, includes two measurement articles and two video demonstrations. They examine different files and different outcomes. Reading their methods closely is more useful than treating their verdicts as a single quality score.

## What the measurements show

WaveDisco’s author regenerated three songs across four model versions, keeping each song’s lyrics and prompt the same, then measured LUFS and True Peak with one analysis pipeline. That setup makes changes among those particular outputs easier to inspect. LUFS and True Peak describe measured properties; neither tells us which version listeners would prefer.

WaveDisco reported no consistent direction from v5.5 to v6 in those measurements. The author also reported that a WAV/MP3 loudness gap disappeared in the v6 comparisons, while stressing that only two songs had both export formats available. For a producer, that is a reason to check the formats they actually plan to use, not a promise that their own exports will match.

ytyng asked a different measurement question. It reported that its v6 exports had 2 to 3.4 dB less energy from 2 to 12 kHz and a 36% lower spectral centroid than its earlier exports. But ytyng says the comparison used two v6 songs and six earlier songs, with different prompts and genres. The v6 files were also m4a exports using Opus, while the earlier files were MP3s. That is ytyng's reported difference in its exported files, not an independently replicated measurement; its cause remains unresolved. Different songs, prompts, and codecs could each matter.

The distinction matters if you hear an export as dull or bright. ytyng says it measured audio *after export*, rather than Suno’s internal master. A useful follow-up is therefore to compare the actual files you might release, using matched formats and repeated generations where you can. The supplied evidence does not show that v6 generally has less high-frequency energy, or that a spectral measurement predicts whether listeners will like a song.

## Control needs its own test

Sound measurements cannot tell you whether Suno kept your melody or followed a vocal direction. In one video, On a Creative Journey tried increasingly detailed directions for a song and judged a later vocal smoother. The creator also acknowledged that repeated generations might explain some of the perceived improvement. The final version received Studio cleanup, adding another change between the initial generation and the finished example. That makes the demonstration useful for seeing one creator’s process, but weak evidence that a particular wording change caused the result.

In another video, Andrea Olsen of Produce Like a Boss said Suno largely followed her directions and retained the melody of one uploaded voice memo. She also said she could still hear an AI quality in the result. Her account addresses a practical question for someone starting from a sung idea: did this output preserve what the creator meant to keep? It remains one creator’s judgment about one memo. Olsen also promotes her own music-business program in the video, a commercial interest readers should know when weighing her evaluation.

Taken together, the articles and videos suggest two separate checks for your own work. First, inspect the exported audio you would actually use, including its level, peaks, and any spectral concern you hear. Second, compare the song with your original intent: melody, vocal direction, and arrangement. Record the prompt, generation, export format, and any later edits so you can tell which version you are judging. This is a proposed testing method drawn from the sources, not a finding that v6 performs consistently across songs.

These reports leave a meaningful gap. Their samples are small, the demonstrations judge individual outputs, and none is a blinded listening study. A broader comparison would need matched prompts and export formats, repeated generations, varied songs, and listeners judging the results. Until then, the release question is local: does *your* chosen export sound right to you, and does it still carry *your* musical idea?
